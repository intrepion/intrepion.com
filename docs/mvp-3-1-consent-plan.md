# MVP 3.1 amendment: consent-based household invitations

Status: **scope, wireframes, mockups, and standalone prototype approved by the owner**. See the [prototype record](../design/mvp-3-1-consent/PROTOTYPE.md). Q1–Q58 in the invitation-consent discussion and the consolidated scope are approved. It amends the direct-email initiation in [MVP 3.1](mvp-3-1-plan.md); all other accepted MVP 3.1 behavior remains unless explicitly changed here. The [exploration](mvp-3-1-consent-exploration.md) preserves the discussion.

## Requesting an invitation

- A current household head creates a reusable request link, valid for seven days, and shares it directly. It is a link to request an invitation; the actual invitation still arrives privately by email. The head is told that anyone who receives the link can pass it along and independently request an invitation.
- Before Google sign-in, show only a generic household-request screen. After sign-in, show the household name, the Google-verified email address and the explicit Request invitation email action. Explain that an invitation may be sent to that address, with technical retries or a head's resend within 30 days. This is not marketing consent. Opening or scanning a link and signing in alone do not submit a request.
- Require a verified Google email and bind the request to both the stable Google identity and that address. If the verified address changes, require a new request to send to the new address. Offer account switching if an email invitation is opened with the wrong Google identity, without consuming the invitation. A suspended account cannot request access. A current head who opens their own household's link sees that they already have access; no request or notice is created.
- A request is pending for seven days. Keep at most one pending request per Google identity per household across links; repeat attempts display the existing request and do not create extra notices. A natural link expiry prevents new requests but leaves already pending requests valid until their own deadline.
- A person can sign in freshly with the same Google identity on My invitation requests to see and withdraw their own pending requests and unaccepted invitations even after the link expires or the browser closes. This limited access does not create a full account or reveal others' requests.

## Head review and invitation delivery

- Current heads see pending requests in Household management, including Google display name, verified email, request time and Approve/Decline controls; never show the Google subject. The first confirmed decision wins under concurrency. A decline appears on My invitation requests without sending a decline email. The same identity cannot request again through that link for seven days; a head can share a new link for an earlier retry.
- Approval issues at most one private invitation to the requested email, subject to sending and safety controls. If the monthly sending ceiling is already reached, approval is not recorded: preserve the pending request, show general unsaved-action feedback and allow retry while the request remains valid. Coordinate reservations and concurrent writes so approvals and notification sends cannot exceed the shared attempt budget. Never claim a send happened merely because it was queued.
- The email invitation retains the approved seven-day, single-use, recipient-bound and explicit-acceptance rules. An existing Intrepion user retains their account and display name. A new account and equal-head membership are created atomically only after explicit acceptance; no household content or History is available to the requester beforehand.
- Technical retries and explicit Send again are allowed until withdrawal or acceptance, but emails may be sent only within 30 days of the original request. Explicit Send again replaces the prior invitation link. A link issued within that 30-day window remains valid for its own seven-day lifetime even if acceptance occurs later. Once 30 days have passed, another email requires a new request.
- The requester may withdraw a pending request. After issuance but before acceptance, withdrawal revokes the invitation and cancels unsent delivery. An accepted membership is unaffected.

## Head notifications

- Request-notification opt-in is explicit, defaults off, and is separate for each head and household. Offer it when creating a request link and in Household settings. Send only to that head's verified Google email; if the address changes, pause sending to the new address until the head confirms it in the app. Opting in later does not generate notifications for older pending requests.
- For each new request, send one notification to each current opted-in head. Include the household name, a generic request notice and a link to review in the authenticated app; keep the requester's name and email in the app. Include a link to the setting where the head can turn notifications off.
- Duplicate attempts on an existing pending request create no new notifications. Use bounded automatic technical retries; an uncertain provider result may cause duplicate delivery, so do not promise exactly-once email. All invitation and notification attempts share the existing 10,000-attempt UTC-month ceiling without separate quotas. A notification failure or exhausted allowance leaves the request visible and reviewable.
- Turning notifications off stops new notifications and cancels that head's queued notices and retries. Existing hard-bounce and complaint suppression applies to notifications; only the site owner can review and clear suppression. Display Delivery not confirmed when the provider has accepted a message but no delivery confirmation exists; show definite failure only with evidence, and provide operational detail to the owner.

## Revocation, pausing and recovery

- Any current head may revoke a household request link. Explicit revocation cancels pending requests through that link; already issued invitations and accepted memberships are unaffected. A natural expiry has the separate behavior described above.
- If a link's creating head leaves, is removed or is suspended, revoke their links and cancel pending requests through them. Other heads may create links. If the head who approved and issued an invitation leaves, is removed or is suspended, revoke their unaccepted invitation and cancel unsent delivery; accepted memberships remain valid.
- Household closure revokes its links and cancels pending requests, unsent notifications and unaccepted invitations. Existing protected closure and household-erasure rules apply.
- The existing site-owner growth pause blocks link creation, new requests, approvals and invitation sending. Hold queued head notifications while paused, canceling them if their request expires, is withdrawn or is decided. Existing lists and requests remain visible; withdrawal, link revocation and notification opt-out remain possible.
- Recovering from an older backup revokes request links, cancels pending requests and unsent notifications, and turns notification opt-ins off; heads can opt in again. Apply the identifying-data retention rules during recovery before activating the restored database. Existing independent protections for account suspensions, head removals, erasures and closure remain in force.

## Privacy, retention and safeguards

- Record the request time, consent-wording version, household, and Google identity and verified address used to request the email. Never retain a Google login token as consent evidence. Retain identifying request details and consent records for 30 days after the request or resulting invitation reaches a final state, then remove them. Keep a household History entry that a request and decision occurred, without the requester's identifying details.
- Keep detailed notification delivery records for the existing 30-day email-log period. Owner Admin may show recipient addresses in existing email-operation records for troubleshooting and may show counts, usage, failures, suppression and abuse flags. Do not expose household content or private request-link secrets in Admin, logs or browser storage.
- Request links, requests, consent records and queued notifications count toward retained storage while they exist. Use configurable hidden safety limits and rate controls, including for links, requests and notifications; exact launch values require measurement. Preserve existing protected erasure and non-numerical ordinary-user feedback.
- Use stable action identities and atomic transactional checks for requests, approvals, withdrawals, revocations and closure. A lost response can be safely retried without duplicate requests, decisions or new sets of notifications. If withdrawal or revocation wins before approval, issue no invitation; if approval wins first, a subsequent withdrawal revokes the unaccepted invitation. Preserve the approved real RPC/local-D1, browser/real-API, encrypted recovery and signed-provider-feedback test boundaries.

## Design and release gates

Approval of this scope does not approve implementation or deployment. Create and approve wireframes, then mockups, then a throwaway standalone prototype that runs locally without the web server. Implement separately after prototype approval, test at the previously approved boundaries and complete fixed-baseline review. Reassess SNS/SQS feedback and dead-letter costs, credentials, provider access and migration; do not submit an AWS production-access acknowledgment until the actual operating process makes every statement true. Measure and approve launch safety settings, and obtain the existing live-rollout approval before deploying or sending production invitations.
