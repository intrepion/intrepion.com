Not another issue tracker.

Linear helps teams execute known software work. Intrepion is for the earlier, messier moment: you have a domain, a goal, and a dozen possible directions, but need a sequence you can actually follow.

Time-horizon discipline

Every roadmap uses 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 1 year, and 1 decade so immediate progress and long-term compounding stay connected.

Tutorial-aware steps

Roadmap items can point to guidance for the choices users make, or mark missing tutorials as planned and requestable by eligible members.

Automation with review

Repeated tutorial work can become services later, but credential access, sensitive actions, and AI-generated workflows require explicit review.

From intention to sequence to execution.

The product center is not task management. It is helping someone move from "I want to launch this" to a visible path of next actions, guidance, and future assistance.

1. Goal

Name the project

Start with a real ambition: a business, portfolio, product, service, or site that needs a concrete launch path.

2. Roadmap

Split the horizons

Separate the next hour from the next decade so strategy does not crowd out the move that can happen today.

3. Tutorial

Attach guidance

Show available tutorials for roadmap choices, and make missing guidance visible instead of silently leaving users stuck.

4. Service

Assist carefully

Turn repeatable tutorial steps into reviewed services only after the safety, credential, and confirmation rules are clear.
